# Review findings: agents/story-review-oathkeeper.md (claude-whisperer, individual review)

1. High. Lines 48, 57, 65, 119. Genre expectations have no limit on what counts as one, so the agent will list beats and tropes (meet-cute, ticking clock, pace) as commitments and report each miss; "never high" caps only confidence while line 119 lets an unserved genre promise be critical. Two examples are themselves formula: "a thriller's pace" (48) and "a book pitched for young readers with no character near their age" (57). Line 47 forbids building commitments from a recalled comparable title while step 2 builds them from a recalled genre. Fix: limit genre expectations to the genre's defining promise, what readers would feel cheated without (a romance ends with the couple together, a mystery's solution is revealed, a cozy keeps violence off the page); at most one or two per genre; say plainly that beats, tropes, structure, pacing and characters' ages are never genre expectations. Remove the thriller-pace and young-readers examples, or move the second under "vague".

2. Medium. Lines 48, 57, 65. Only the statement can excuse a broken genre convention, so an outline note such as "ending deliberately bittersweet" does not count and the agent reports the author's own recorded choice. Fix: add to Judgment: "Where the bible itself says a genre convention is broken on purpose, treat that as part of the statement and drop the expectation."

3. Medium. Lines 48, 57, 59, 78. The whodunit fair-clue example is a payoff never set up, and "a thriller's pace" and the four-generations pacing conflict are proportion; all are structure's ground. Line 78's "an ending that breaks the stated genre's promise" collides with the dramaturg, which reports a mystery ending that never names the killer. Fix: replace those examples with cases that are only about what kind of book it is. Extend line 78: "a payoff the outline never sets up, including a mystery's clue or solution, is structure's; yours is an ending that breaks a genre promise it does set up, such as a romance that ends apart."

4. Medium. Lines 47, 91, 128. Line 128 defines low confidence as a finding that "depends on one reading of a vague commitment", contradicting lines 47 and 91 (a vague commitment honoured under one reasonable reading is not a finding). Fix: remove "one reading of a vague commitment" from line 128; keep only the canon-versus-note kind of assumption.

5. Medium. Lines 35, 38, 41-43. The test for "intent, not story material" fails at the top of the bible, where a synopsis of events can be taken as the statement; and there is no floor for a thin statement such as bare genre labels. Fix: add one line saying a summary of events is story material even at the top of the bible, and a logline counts only for the premise commitment; and another saying that when the statement is genre labels alone, the statement line says so and the review checks only the genre's defining promise.

6. Low. Lines 35-41. "Look for it in this order, highest authority first" reads as stop at the first hit, so commitments in lower sources are dropped. Fix: "Gather it from all three; where they disagree, follow the higher one."

7. Low. Lines 17, 39. Source 3 "project instruction files in your context" may include the user-level CLAUDE.md, which can describe a different book. Fix: "the project's own CLAUDE.md, not user-level instructions that apply to every project".

8. Low. Line 25. The manuscript scope rule says "gaps inside the bible", which is coverage's word. Fix: "departures already in the bible".
